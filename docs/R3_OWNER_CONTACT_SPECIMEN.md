# R3 OWNER CONTACT SPECIMEN — Human-Observation Campaign

**Date:** 2026-09-25  
**Status:** active implementation / pre-Owner observation  
**Parent mechanism:** RELATIONAL MANIFOLD R3  
**Classification:** instrumentation + human translation, not R4 and not a combat organism

## 0. Purpose

R3 is mechanically qualified as a joint-afterstate causal kernel, but no human has yet established that the phenomenon is readable, controllable, interesting or worth carrying into an adversarial experiment.

This campaign asks a smaller question before R4:

> **When the qualified R3 relation is placed under direct human control, can the Owner actually perceive and manipulate a shared material relation whose history affects what happens next?**

Hard boundary:

> **R3 kernel qualified ≠ R3 human phenomenon qualified.**

## 1. Hypothesis card

### Player action

The Owner:

- moves one embodied participant with ordinary WASD locomotion;
- continuously points GUIDE with the mouse;
- requests COMMIT with left click;
- may move into contact, remain in contact, redirect, move away and immediately act again;
- resets manually with R.

There is no special bind / parry / escape input.

### Realized behavior

The specimen restores the exact qualified R3 tool-contact kernel.

The second tool receives a slow deterministic guide rhythm that depends only on simulation time. It does not inspect the player's state, does not choose tactics and does not cooperate with contact.

Material contact may change both tool states. No automatic neutral reset is introduced.

### 30-second discriminator

Without reading a combat tutorial, the Owner should be able to notice whether:

- tool contact behaves like a material shared relation rather than a visual overlap;
- guiding / moving while contact exists changes the relation;
- breaking contact can leave a non-neutral inherited tool state;
- a next COMMIT begins from that actual inherited state.

This does not need to be "fun" to be perceptible.

### Feniks relevance

If a mechanically valid shared afterstate is not legible or usable under direct control, adding adversarial pressure is more likely to manufacture meaning than discover it.

If the phenomenon is perceptible and invites manipulation, R4 becomes justified as a new question rather than implementation momentum.

### Falsifier

Human-facing R3 fails or is demoted to donor-only if the first observation is dominated by any of these:

- contact is effectively invisible;
- contact feels arbitrary rather than caused;
- the other tool reads as a dead wall;
- relation management feels mainly sticky / fiddly / like physics wrestling;
- useful control requires explanatory HUD semantics;
- inherited state is technically present but not perceptibly relevant;
- the specimen needs force tuning, stamina, parry windows or opponent scripting before it becomes intelligible.

## 2. Contamination boundary

Before first Owner observation, do not add:

- HP;
- damage;
- stamina;
- parry timing;
- bind mode;
- hidden advantage state;
- contact win/loss labels;
- target-aware or mechanic-aware AI;
- authored "correct" response;
- feel tuning intended to make the mechanism enjoyable.

Allowed presentation work:

- canvas rendering of bodies and real material tools;
- contact point;
- short tool-tip traces;
- raw contact on/off and duration;
- direct control instructions;
- exact source SHA / provenance.

## 3. Partner contract

The second participant is deliberately not an opponent in this campaign.

Its body is stationary and its material tool runs ordinary GUIDE toward a slow deterministic time function.

This avoids two false positives:

1. a dead static wall making one-sided resistance look relational;
2. an adversarial script manufacturing situations that make R3 look useful.

The tool still has the same finite authority and contact law as the player tool, so contact can alter its state.

## 4. Machine gate before Owner observation

Required:

- the preserved R3 deterministic suite remains green;
- Owner-control helper tests remain green;
- browser module loads without runtime errors;
- controls mutate the same R3 state used by the regression suite;
- no automatic neutralization is present;
- deployment identifies the exact source SHA.

Machine status vocabulary:

- **kernel fidelity:** PASS / FAIL;
- **input/runtime:** PASS / FAIL;
- **observability plumbing:** PASS / FAIL;
- **human phenomenon / feel:** always UNPROVEN until Owner play.

## 5. Owner gate

The first Owner pass should be minimally leading.

Initial instruction should be no more than:

> Move with WASD, aim the tool with the mouse, left-click to commit, R to reset. Play with contact and tell me what you notice.

Do not ask the Owner to "look for R3" or explain the intended result before raw reaction.

## 6. Decision after Owner evidence

- **Imperceptible but mechanically intact:** investigate control/presentation translation before blaming or promoting the kernel.
- **Perceptible but chore-like / sticky / arbitrary:** preserve the causal donor and stop treating R3 as a promising human combat direction.
- **Perceptible and interesting:** open a separate R4 hypothesis for minimal adversarial pressure.
- **Unexpected behavior dominates:** follow that evidence instead of forcing the R4 roadmap.
