# Cross-Campaign Combat Discovery Audit — Exchange Before Consequence

> **HISTORICAL RECORD — NOT CURRENT AUTHORITY.**
> This file preserves the research state at its date. Any `active`, `current` or `next` wording below is historical. Current truth lives in `README.md` and `docs/RESEARCH_STATE.md`.

**Date:** 2026-09-24  
**Status:** active synthesis; no implementation authority  
**Scope:** Combat Lab -> Feniks combat discovery  
**Owner play:** not requested

## 0. Why this audit exists

Combat Lab has now falsified several materially different whole-organism attempts without finding a combat language worth spending more Owner attention on.

This is not evidence that Feniks combat is impossible.

It is evidence that another immediate weapon archetype is likely to repeat the same structural mistake.

The strongest recurring pattern is:

> **we repeatedly build mechanically valid spatial relations, then ordinary offense or unconstrained disengagement bypasses them before they become the fight.**

The next research object should therefore move one level above sword / spear / shield / projectile choice.

Primary question:

> **What must a Feniks combat exchange make the player perceive, choose and commit to before consequence, so offense, movement and world relations cannot trivially bypass one another?**

## 1. Evidence ledger

### Ruined Gate / Combat Terrarium

What survived:

- responsive movement during attacks;
- causal weapon/world contact;
- directional body-motion contribution to impact;
- same-step symmetric hit authority;
- real sword/spear clearance differences;
- positive Owner signal for weight;
- positive Owner signal for relatively fast, meaningful consequence.

What failed:

- Owner judged the organism effectively unplayable / not worth tuning;
- close engagement collapsed into adhesive melee swirl;
- world geometry existed but rarely became a meaningful decision;
- better hit feedback explained consequence without creating richer agency;
- spear strategy could be proven by a competent controller without becoming naturally compelling play.

Key lesson:

> **causal correctness and readable consequence do not create a possibility surface by themselves.**

### Phenotype Combat Ecology P0/P1

What survived:

- body/equipment mass can flow through shared movement/contact rules;
- directional shield geometry can be material rather than hidden damage reduction;
- brace can trade movement/turn authority for contact resistance;
- body envelope can matter to world access;
- a continuity hybrid can interpolate without a hard class flag.

What failed as product evidence:

- strategy crossover depended too heavily on authored route/policy structure;
- body-size access risked becoming the experiment's answer rather than an emergent consequence;
- heavy identity remained vulnerable to "same fighter, slower/harder to move".

Key lesson:

> **role differentiation is not enough when the situation pre-authors the role's answer.**

### O1 HOLD / BREAK

What survived:

- frontal shield geometry can consume a committed damage path without binary parry;
- both bodies can continue to displace under contact;
- brace can change frontal yield without global infinite mass;
- side/rear committed contact naturally bypasses frontal coverage;
- movement can escape readable threat geometry without i-frames;
- real multi-angle concurrency can make contact support matter.

What failed:

- stance relevance appeared only in a narrow sustained-concurrency regime;
- prior success depended materially on multi-target cleave;
- after single-contact restriction, the robust HOLD / BREAK distinction disappeared;
- proactive braced shield-drive without damage did not create useful BREAK.

Key lesson:

> **defensive contact can be real yet still fail to produce a reusable exchange when offense either erases pressure or contact itself has no productive follow-through.**

### O2 REACH / THREAT

What survived:

- persistent spear geometry;
- outer-tip single-contact thrust;
- no passive damage authority;
- wall truncation;
- zero-damage close clearance;
- live locomotion;
- same-step hostile commitment survives player resolution.

What failed:

- with slow pursuit, retreat cancels engagement;
- with faster pursuit, direct approach feeds bodies into the spear axis;
- across the sweep, backwards movement + thrust remains safest / simplest;
- no broad pursuit envelope produces richer lane/threat play.

Key lesson:

> **range is not strategy if backing away can either cancel engagement or turn pursuit into an automatic delivery mechanism.**

### Adversarial Combat Organism v1

What survived:

- light dash-line and heavy sweep-arc can create genuinely different movement responses;
- one shared prepare -> commit -> recover lifecycle can support action diversity;
- first-solid-body adversary authority;
- material body screening;
- bounded screen tolerance;
- screen value can exist with zero friendly damage;
- some screen value survives coarse positional perception.

What failed:

- mixed pair remained largely two independent attack relations / health bars;
- focus order barely mattered;
- material cross-interaction was sparse in ordinary fights;
- when normal compact offense was restored, body-screen authority produced **0 / 24** combat-outcome improvements.

Key lesson:

> **a real material relation can disappear as soon as ordinary offense deletes the actors carrying that relation.**

### LINE / IMPULSE v0

What survived:

- finite projectile travel;
- swept collision / no tunneling;
- first-solid-body / wall truth;
- deterministic draw/release with live movement;
- bounded mass-dependent impulse;
- no hidden stun or forced attack cancel;
- same-step projectile and hostile commitment can coexist.

What failed:

- stand + fire and max-rate fire deleted the rusher before engagement;
- backwards kite + lethal fire cleared safely;
- damage-only and damage+impulse produced effectively the same lethal outcomes;
- impulse-only prepare/commit timing did not create additional missed commits;
- backwards retreat + repeated zero-damage impulse suppressed engagement for 10 seconds at full player HP.

Key lesson:

> **ranged consequence either became HP deletion or, without damage, a way to refuse engagement rather than negotiate it.**

## 2. Cross-campaign failure pattern

The evidence supports five recurring failure families.

### 2.1 Resolution-before-negotiation

Player offense is usually available as soon as input and geometry permit.

That means:

- the player often does not need to earn an opening;
- body/world relations can be skipped by simply resolving damage sooner;
- spatial mechanisms become secondary modifiers around an always-available damage loop.

This does **not** imply arbitrary attack cooldowns or animation locks are needed.

It implies the relationship between **readiness, position, body state, weapon state and consequence** is underdeveloped.

### 2.2 Retreat can cancel engagement for free

When the local objective is effectively:

> kill them while staying alive

retreat is often rational if:

- movement remains responsive;
- the world is open enough;
- enemies do not have teleport/cheat pursuit;
- ranged/reach tools remain usable while moving.

Artificial fixes such as stamina tax, backwards-speed penalties or invisible arena pressure would attack the symptom.

A stronger Feniks-compatible question is:

> **what does retreat voluntarily concede?**

Possible concessions can be world-grounded:

- position;
- access;
- line;
- protection of another actor;
- initiative;
- control of a useful relation;
- progress toward a local objective.

Retreat should remain legal and responsive.

It should not always be free.

### 2.3 Mechanically real defense often has no productive afterstate

Shield contact, body screening, clearance and impulse can all prevent or alter one event.

But a prevented event is not yet a combat language.

A useful exchange should leave a meaningful next state:

- attacker displaced / overextended;
- defender yielded but preserved a line;
- weapon line changed;
- one actor gained angle;
- one actor lost access;
- a route opened;
- a threat became exposed;
- an ally gained a window.

Without such afterstate, defense becomes delay.

Then offense resumes unchanged.

### 2.4 HP-clear compresses the possibility surface

Fast lethality itself is not rejected.

Owner feedback explicitly favored meaningful, relatively fast hits.

The problem is different:

> **when damage is both the easiest action and the only durable progress, every other relation is pressured to justify itself only by how quickly it helps kill.**

That makes:

- body holding;
- screening;
- displacement;
- line creation;
- terrain;
- threat;

fragile, because killing the source of pressure is usually more direct.

### 2.5 Whole-organism discovery still lacked an exchange grammar

We varied:

- body;
- shield;
- stance;
- weapon reach;
- clearance;
- adversary action geometry;
- body screening;
- projectile travel;
- impulse.

Yet most fights still reduced toward:

> move until attack is available -> deal consequence -> repeat / retreat.

This suggests the missing object is not one more mechanic.

Candidate missing object:

> **a reusable exchange grammar connecting intent, contested relation, commitment, consequence and afterstate.**

## 3. New working thesis — EXCHANGE BEFORE CONSEQUENCE

This is a research hypothesis, not a Feniks design decision.

A valuable combat exchange may need five linked stages.

### A. PERCEIVE

Before consequence, the player can read something materially relevant:

- opponent intent / approach;
- current threat geometry;
- body / equipment relation;
- world relation;
- what local value is at risk.

No omniscient HUD solution is assumed.

### B. CHOOSE

The player has multiple legal responses before contact:

- hold;
- yield;
- angle;
- threaten;
- intercept;
- reposition;
- give ground;
- contest access;
- intentionally accept one relation to gain another.

The response should not be pre-authored by one route.

### C. COMMIT

A chosen action changes the player's relation to the next moment.

Commitment should come from physical/gameplay consequence, not arbitrary `canMove=false`.

Possible future forms:

- weapon line / recovery;
- occupied hands / coverage;
- body balance / support;
- facing authority;
- momentum;
- stance transition;
- preparation;
- exposure of another angle.

The exact form is open.

### D. RESOLVE

World truth decides what actually happened:

- miss;
- contact;
- interception;
- displacement;
- blocked line;
- partial / glancing contact;
- clean damaging hit.

Resolution should preserve same-step symmetry and first-solid-body truth where relevant.

### E. INHERIT

The result changes the next spatial decision before the loop resets.

This is the currently weakest stage in most failed organisms.

Examples of meaningful inherited state:

- new separation;
- overextension;
- lost / gained angle;
- occupied / released space;
- weapon displaced from a useful line;
- body yielded from a choke;
- temporary exposure caused by the previous physical result;
- an ally / route / objective becoming safer or more vulnerable.

The exchange should not simply return to the same neutral pursuit state after every event.

## 4. Fast consequence is compatible with richer exchange

Do **not** infer that Feniks now needs sponge enemies or long duels.

A stronger working model is:

> **low-frequency, high-meaning clean consequence.**

A clean hit may still matter greatly.

The research target is to make clean consequence something that emerges from a won relation rather than the default always-available action.

This preserves the Owner-positive signal for weight / meaningful hits while protecting possibility richness.

---

## 5. Engagement should contest local value, not only HP

A major audit finding is that a purely survival/kill objective structurally rewards disengagement.

The next research lane should therefore test a minimal **contested value**.

This is not a mission system.

It is a local reason that makes space matter.

Candidate value can be:

- access through a relation;
- control of a useful region;
- protection of another body / object;
- stopping another body from reaching something;
- preserving a firing / movement line;
- maintaining route continuity.

The key rule:

> **retreat may remain safe, but it must visibly concede something the player can care about.**

Avoid:

- invisible retreat tax;
- stamina punishment;
- arbitrary backwards-speed reduction;
- shrinking arena;
- scripted "stand here" success zone that pre-authors one answer.

---

## 6. Why this is compatible with Feniks

### Responsive movement

Movement remains available.

The hypothesis does not require universal animation lock.

### No universal dodge / i-frames

Avoidance can still come from leaving real threat geometry.

### Physical consequence without simulation fetish

Only material facts that improve decisions need survive:

- body;
- occupied space;
- support;
- weapon line;
- mass;
- contact;
- obstruction;
- displacement.

### Classless specialization

An exchange grammar creates useful axes for specialization without class flags:

- who can hold;
- who can yield/recover quickly;
- who can threaten at reach;
- who can reposition under burden;
- who can maintain coverage;
- who can exploit an opening;
- who can create one for someone else.

### Progression

Progression can deepen an existing exchange instead of only scaling DPS:

- faster recovery from overextension;
- better control of a heavy weapon;
- stronger contact support;
- more movement authority under armour;
- better preparation;
- new learned responses / techniques.

### Terrain

Terrain becomes part of what is contested, not collision scenery added after combat already works.

### Co-op

A shared exchange grammar can naturally support:

- one actor creating/holding a relation;
- another exploiting the opening;
- one actor yielding space to protect another;
- body screening;
- asymmetric equipment roles.

This is more promising than proving isolated solo DPS rotations first and adding party play later.

---

## 7. Anti-goals for the next lane

Do not solve the audit by adding:

- stamina merely to punish retreat;
- global attack cooldown merely to slow damage;
- HP inflation;
- universal dodge;
- binary parry minigame;
- hidden threat-respect AI;
- target-lock ownership;
- one "correct" choke;
- one class-specific defensive switch;
- a score/combo system that substitutes for world consequence.

Do not assume:

- every attack requires long windup;
- every hit requires a formal opening token;
- every exchange must be symmetrical;
- every combat style shares one tempo.

The thesis is about **earned relation**, not about importing fencing rules.

## 8. Next research object — EXCHANGE GRAMMAR v0

Do not implement another full weapon organism yet.

First define a small whole situation that can answer:

> **Can a player and adversary negotiate a valuable local relation through readable intent, movement, commitment and material consequence before damage is allowed to dominate?**

### E0 — no-HP exchange diagnostic

Temporary diagnostic rule:

- no HP win condition;
- no kill / clear objective;
- consequence is spatial / positional;
- one local contested value;
- one readable adversary commitment;
- player retains responsive locomotion;
- no dodge / i-frames;
- no hidden class authority.

This is deliberately a diagnostic, not a proposed final Feniks damage model.

Required outcome:

- at least three materially different legal responses;
- retreat safely concedes local value rather than ending the experiment;
- one successful defensive response creates a useful afterstate, not just delay;
- aggressive input without winning a relation does not automatically dominate;
- movement alone cannot infinitely suppress all engagement while also preserving the local value.

### E1 — earned consequence reintroduction

Only if E0 produces a rich exchange.

Reintroduce high-consequence damage **without changing the exchange grammar**.

Red-team:

- does damage again erase the spatial relation?
- does click-spam become dominant?
- does retreat become dominant?
- does defensive success create real offense opportunity?
- do clean hits remain relatively rare and meaningful?

If E1 collapses, the exchange model did not actually survive consequence.

### E2 — phenotype / weapon donor pressure

Only after E1.

Pressure the same exchange with selected donors:

- heavy contact / shield;
- reach;
- distinct adversary actions;
- first-solid-body screening;
- projectile/line relation.

The goal is not to combine everything.

The goal is to see whether one exchange grammar can host materially different bodies / equipment without special-case classes.

## 9. Pre-implementation questions still open

Do not code E0 until these are resolved well enough to state a sharp discriminator:

1. **What local value is contested?**
   It must make retreat meaningful without becoming a scripted capture-point game.

2. **What creates commitment without animation lock?**
   Candidate mechanisms must remain responsive and physically/gameplay-legible.

3. **What is the minimum meaningful afterstate?**
   The result of one exchange must make the next choice different.

4. **What counts as winning a relation before HP exists?**
   The diagnostic needs measurable truth without turning into arbitrary scoring.

5. **How does adversary intent remain readable but not deterministic?**
   The player should read possibilities, not memorize a telegraph puzzle.

These questions are now higher priority than implementation.

---

## 10. Promotion rule

Do not promote EXCHANGE GRAMMAR v0 because a mechanism matrix looks clean.

Before Owner play, it must demonstrate agent-side that:

- multiple responses exist;
- one response does not dominate by policy construction;
- retreat has a real world-grounded concession;
- aggression cannot bypass the exchange;
- consequence leaves a nontrivial afterstate;
- the situation is not a movement puzzle with combat removed.

Only then build a minimal playable organism.

---

## Working invariant

> **Damage should confirm a won combat relation, not replace the need to win one.**
