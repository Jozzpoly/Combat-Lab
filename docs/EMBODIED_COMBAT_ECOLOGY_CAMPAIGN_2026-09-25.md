# Combat Lab vNext — Embodied Combat Ecology Campaign

**Date:** 2026-09-25  
**Status:** active campaign design after first positive S0 Owner test  
**Trigger:** Owner confirms S0 is in the direction Feniks needs while explicitly calling it raw and far from final needs

## 0. Campaign purpose

The next campaign is not "improve the circles".

It is also not "build the new combat system".

The campaign exists to discover which parts of **embodiment** deserve to become durable Feniks combat substrate before divergent weapons, bows, magic, shields and techniques are reintroduced.

Core question:

> **Which body / locomotion / mass / occupied-space relations produce useful, understandable and permissive combat possibilities across many combat modalities?**

## 1. Campaign constraints

The campaign must not:

- turn body size into hidden classes;
- equate large with tank by definition;
- equate small with rogue by definition;
- hardcode one corridor puzzle;
- use HP/damage to manufacture significance too early;
- reintroduce a generic attack engine as the substrate;
- treat realistic physics as the goal;
- preserve S0's formulas just because the Owner liked the phenomenon;
- eliminate weapon/contact donors permanently.

The campaign should preserve:

- direct responsive control;
- material world-grounded constraints;
- alternate routes where physically plausible;
- continuous properties where useful;
- permissive unusual combinations;
- early Owner observation;
- strict separation between causal mechanism evidence and experiential evidence.

## 2. Campaign structure

This is a sequence of research fronts, not a feature roadmap.

### FRONT A — Envelope / topology

Question:

> What kinds of body envelope differences create useful world possibilities without reducing the game to fit/not-fit doors?

Research variables:

- overall scale;
- width vs length / profile;
- orientation;
- multiple geometry families;
- open spaces, chokes and bypasses;
- crowd occupancy.

Required falsifier:

If body size only matters at deliberately authored gaps, the front fails.

### FRONT B — Disentangle size, mass and locomotor authority

This is the immediate highest-information front.

Current S0 confounds:

- radius;
- mass;
- acceleration;
- braking;
- max speed.

Build an orthogonal field where at least:

- same envelope / different mass;
- different envelope / same mass;
- same mass / different locomotor authority;
- unusual combinations

can be compared.

Primary question:

> Which perceived "big / heavy / small / agile" differences come from geometry, inertia/contact, and motor capability respectively?

Do not add combat attacks here.

### FRONT C — Posture / stance-dependent occupied space

Test whether one body can materially change relation to the world without becoming another class.

Examples worth testing:

- brace / hold;
- relaxed / yielding;
- squeeze / narrow passage;
- lowered / compact posture.

Important:

Stance should change material relations, not merely apply a hidden stat modifier.

This front is a direct bridge toward the Owner's Feniks interest in stance-dependent collision.

### FRONT D — Equipment as body extension

Only after body variables are separated.

Test equipment that changes:

- envelope;
- mass;
- carried center / handling;
- persistent coverage;
- clearance.

Candidate donors:

- shield;
- long pole;
- bulky carried object;
- armour / pack burden.

No need for damage yet.

Question:

> Does equipment change how the actor inhabits space before it changes attack numbers?

### FRONT E — Active multi-body pressure

Replace passive drifters with the smallest transparent active bodies necessary to create spatial pressure.

Not enemies with a full combat AI.

Potential relations:

- passing against opposing flow;
- protecting a space / body;
- two bodies contesting a line;
- moving through a crowd;
- supporting / screening another actor.

Goal:

find whether embodiment changes **what the Owner chooses**, not merely trajectories.

### FRONT F — Divergent combat modalities return

Only after the body/world substrate has survived Owner pressure.

Open intentionally different branches:

- compact melee / contact;
- polearm / reach;
- bow / projectile / line of sight;
- shield / occupied coverage;
- magic / world relation.

The old R3, spear, shield and projectile findings become donor candidates here.

The requirement is not that all modalities share mechanics.

It is that all meet the same material actor/world truth where appropriate.

## 3. Immediate next experiment

Working name:

> **S1 — ORTHOGONAL BODY FIELD**

Do not implement it as a giant matrix UI.

It should remain playable and directly manipulable.

Minimum useful comparisons:

1. same size, light vs heavy;
2. same mass, small vs large;
3. same size/mass, weak vs strong locomotor authority;
4. at least one intentionally "weird" combination such as small-heavy or large-light.

The Owner should be able to swap these quickly in the same environment.

Keep the S0 world initially only as one reference condition.

Add at least one second geometry family so the result cannot be explained entirely by the central choke.

## 4. S1 30-second discriminator

Without reading telemetry, the Owner should be able to notice different answers to questions such as:

- Can I fit?
- Can I hold my line?
- Do I get moved?
- Can I move another body?
- How easily can I redirect myself?
- Is my body awkward because of size or because of inertia?

The experiment succeeds if those answers begin separating.

It fails if every phenotype still reads as:

> bigger circle = slower stronger circle.

## 5. Instrumentation required before S1 Owner gate

Machine-side:

- exact phenotype parameters in snapshots;
- deterministic reset;
- same-scene rapid phenotype switching;
- no hidden class branch;
- controlled paired contact scenarios;
- replayable raw-input or fixed-intent comparisons where relevant.

Owner-facing:

- minimal controls;
- no optimization dashboard;
- no labels like "tank", "rogue", "heavy";
- neutral parameter descriptions only if needed after free play.

## 6. Campaign evidence discipline

Promotion ladder:

**MECHANISM**

A parameter really changes the claimed material relation.

**HUMAN-DISCRIMINABLE**

Owner perceives the difference without telemetry dependence.

**DECISION-RELEVANT**

Difference changes what the Owner intentionally does.

**CROSS-MODAL**

Relation still matters when different combat modalities arrive.

**FENIKS CANDIDATE**

Only after it survives multiple organisms and Owner tests.

S0 is currently:

- mechanism: PASS;
- human-discriminable: early PASS / positive signal;
- decision-relevant: partial evidence through route choice, not yet combat-qualified;
- cross-modal: UNPROVEN;
- Feniks candidate semantics: UNPROVEN.

## 7. Explicit stop conditions

Stop / rethink the campaign if:

- body research becomes a movement simulator detached from combat possibility;
- every result is authored by one obstacle;
- mass realism starts outranking control quality;
- unusual combinations are prohibited to protect a role model;
- body variables become a stat spreadsheet rather than playable relations;
- Owner starts debugging the apparatus instead of exploring phenomena;
- we delay Owner play behind large machine-only campaigns again.

## 8. Strategic interpretation

The campaign is deliberately upstream of weapons, but it is not anti-weapon.

The target is a hybrid combat language discovered incrementally.

A future Feniks exchange may combine:

- body occupancy;
- movement;
- stance;
- terrain;
- equipment;
- physical contact;
- projectiles;
- magic;
- authored techniques;
- fast damage when earned.

Combat Lab should discover which combination deserves support rather than decide the combination in advance.

Working invariant:

> **First establish what the character materially is in the world. Then discover what fighting through that body can become.**
