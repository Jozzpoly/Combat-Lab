## 0. Purpose

R0 proved that contact history can persist as continuous readiness and materially change an identical next action.

R1 asks the next question:

> **When a readable adversarial commitment creates real urgency, does inherited readiness change what the player should intentionally do next?**

R1 is still a mechanism cell.

It is not:

- a duel;
- a weapon balance test;
- a parry system;
- an Owner specimen.

No HP or damage exists in R1.

---

## 1. Crossed experimental structure

R1 uses two independent axes.

### Readiness history

The player enters pressure from one of two causal R0 histories:

- **FREE history** — prior COMMIT completed without wall contact;
- **WALL history** — identical prior COMMIT was materially intercepted by a wall.

No readiness state is hand-authored as "left guard" / "right guard".

The actual continuous pose/velocity produced by R0 is reused.

### Pressure side

One light rusher approaches from:

- **EAST**;
- **WEST**.

The pressure geometry is mirrored.

This creates a 2 × 2 base relation:

- FREE × EAST;
- FREE × WEST;
- WALL × EAST;
- WALL × WEST.

Purpose:

> **prevent one inherited pose from becoming a globally best guard and force readiness value to be contextual.**

---

## 2. Adversary donor — committed dash-line

Reuse only the defended A1 concept:

> **readable prepare -> captured dash direction -> committed line -> recover**

The rusher:

- begins at visible distance;
- approaches / settles if needed;
- has one readable prepare window;
- captures direction once;
- does not home during commit;
- continues physically after tool contact unless displacement changes its realized path;
- does not receive hidden stun / parry / cancel authority.

No generic face-hug pursuit loop is needed after the single pulse.

R1 evaluates one bounded pressure exchange.

## 3. Player action surface

Reuse R0:

- responsive locomotion;
- GUIDE through aim intent;
- COMMIT from actual inherited readiness;
- no auto-neutral;
- no dodge;
- no block/parry button;
- no target lock;
- no stamina.

The player is not given a special "intercept" input.

Weapon/body contact during COMMIT is simply a material consequence of geometry.

## 4. Player tool authority

R0 had wall contact but no actor contact.

R1 adds one bounded body-contact law during COMMIT.

Required:

- only active COMMIT can produce deliberate tool/body authority;
- first solid body contact consumes that COMMIT contact;
- contact point / current tool direction are real;
- contact applies bounded displacement to the rusher and reciprocal weapon/body consequence where needed;
- no damage;
- no stun flag;
- no forced rusher state cancel;
- dash remains committed to its captured line.

A successful tool contact can make the rusher miss only if:

> **material displacement changes the actual dash geometry enough.**

This is not binary parry authority.

## 6. Response families

These are agent-side probes, not future instructions to Owner.

### IMMEDIATE COMMIT

Aim at the rusher and COMMIT as soon as pressure is readable.

Question:

> **does inherited readiness make this good in some crossed cells and bad in others?**

### GUIDE THEN COMMIT

Use ordinary GUIDE first, then COMMIT when the tool has moved toward a useful relation.

Question:

> **can intentional conversion repair an initially bad readiness state inside the real warning window?**

### LATERAL EVADE

No tool authority.

Use responsive locomotion to leave the locked dash line.

Question:

> **does movement remain a legal separate answer, and how much spatial concession does it require?**

### GUIDE + MOVE + COMMIT

Only after the simpler probes.

Question:

> **can readiness and locomotion cooperate without one deleting the other?**

Do not introduce more policy families merely to find a winner.

## 7. Desired phenomenon

R1 is interesting only if the crossed matrix produces contextual action value.

Example shape, not required exact winner:

- WALL-derived readiness may make immediate EAST interception plausible;
- the same state may be poor against WEST;
- FREE-derived readiness may reverse that relation;
- GUIDE THEN COMMIT may repair a bad state at a time/opportunity cost;
- LATERAL EVADE remains legal regardless of tool readiness.

The actual result may differ.

The key is:

> **history should change which response is locally attractive, not merely how many milliseconds one animation takes.**

## 10. Movement-only baseline

Mandatory.

Run the same EAST/WEST pressure with:

- no tool/body authority;
- lateral movement only.

> **R1 pressure is too weak to reveal readiness value.**

Do not fix that with stamina or slower locomotion.

## 19. R1 result — FAIL AS PRESSURE CELL, R0 READINESS DONOR SURVIVES

Final evidence checkpoint:

- mirrored pressure cell: `df27aa139e4489fa8e152a348a135610f527532e`;
- contact timing / material sweep: `92c231f57635076b54e189b2770c2479d0e85a2d`;
- deterministic suite: **19 / 19 PASS**;
- Owner play: **NOT REQUESTED**.

### 19.1 K0/K1 substrate behaved as intended

Qualified narrow facts:

- EAST/WEST pressure is mirrored;
- rusher has readable prepare;
- dash direction captures once and does not home;
- no HP/damage model exists;
- player locomotion remains live;
- COMMIT-only tool/body contact exists;
- tool contact does not cancel `rusher.mode = commit`;
- same-step body contact cannot be retroactively erased by tool consequence;
- zero material displacement can preserve the contact event while removing velocity consequence.

Thus the failure below is not caused by hidden parry authority.

### 19.2 Readiness changes contact geometry

Base crossed matrix, immediate COMMIT:

**FREE × EAST**
- tool contacts: 0;
- player body contact: yes.

**FREE × WEST**
- tool contacts: 1;
- player body contact: yes.

**WALL × EAST**
- tool contacts: 1;
- player body contact: yes.

**WALL × WEST**
- tool contacts: 1;
- player body contact: yes.

Therefore inherited readiness is not semantically dead under pressure.

It changes which material tool/body contacts actually occur.

### 19.3 GUIDE did not change the pressure outcome

GUIDE THEN COMMIT produced the same player-body-contact outcome in all four crossed cells.

It changed some local contact geometry / displacement, but did not create a different useful response.

This means:

> **R1 did not demonstrate that intentional readiness conversion changes what the player should do next.**

### 19.4 Movement remains a real independent answer

LATERAL EVADE:

- body miss in all tested mirrored cells;
- no tool contact;
- ~107.95 units maximum player displacement.

This is useful evidence:

- the dash is not unavoidable;
- no dodge/i-frame state is required;
- ordinary movement can leave the locked threat geometry.

It also means the pressure cell has one clearly effective response while the readiness responses fail to alter outcome.

### 19.5 Material contact is real but not consequence-bearing enough

Broad material-authority sweep used immediate COMMIT in all four history × side cells.

At material scale:

- 0.00: 0 / 4 body misses;
- 0.35: 0 / 4;
- 0.70: 0 / 4;
- 1.00: 0 / 4.

At full material law:

- FREE × WEST lateral rusher deviation: ~9.15;
- WALL × EAST: ~11.17;
- WALL × WEST: ~12.89.

The designed material law caps rusher delta speed at **105**.

Therefore scales 1.4 / 2 / 3 / 4 correctly saturate at the same authority and still produce:

- **0 / 4 body misses**.

This is important.

The sweep does not say contact was fake.

It says:

> **within the predesigned bounded material law, tool contact changes trajectory but not the pressure result.**

Running more attribution against the same failed consequence would optimize a mechanism cell around a missing phenomenon.

Stop here.

### 19.8 Product interpretation

R1 accidentally asked continuous readiness to earn itself as:

> **a defensive interruption / parry-like way to nullify an incoming body commitment.**

That is not required by the R0 hypothesis and may be a poor use of readiness.

The stronger remaining hypothesis is different:

> **persistent readiness may matter more in exploiting the afterstate created by an exchange than in canceling the exchange before it happens.**

Example research pressure:

- player avoids / survives committed geometry through ordinary movement;
- adversary's own commitment leaves real overextension / separation / orientation;
- player's inherited weapon readiness determines which follow-up relation is naturally available;
- no hidden "vulnerable" token is required.

This should be tested before increasing interception force.
